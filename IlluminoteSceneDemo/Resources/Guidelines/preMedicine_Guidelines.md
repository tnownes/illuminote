### Medical School Personal Statement Guidelines

**Overall Assessment**

* **The "Why Medicine" Narrative:** Evaluate whether the central motivation is clear, credible, and sustained across the full statement.
* **Style and Voice:** Assess for authentic, warm, and professional tone that balances confidence with humility.
* **Language Quality:** Flag inflated diction, excessive abstraction, and jargon-heavy explanations that reduce accessibility.
* **Reflection Depth:** Prioritize evidence of insight, growth, and evolving professional identity over simple activity descriptions.

**Opening Paragraph**

* **Distinct Hook:** Check if the opening offers a specific perspective tied to real experience rather than generic aspiration.
* **Cliché Detection:** Flag broad claims like "I want to help people" unless paired with concrete context and reflection.
* **Direction Setting:** Confirm the opening establishes a trajectory that the rest of the essay actually develops.

**Body Paragraphs**

* **Reflection Over Recitation:** Ensure body sections do not read as a narrative CV. Reward interpretation, not listing.
* **Core Competencies:** Look for behavioral evidence of empathy, integrity, teamwork, cultural humility, and ethical judgment.
* **Clinical Readiness Signals:** Check for concrete examples showing comfort with uncertainty, service orientation, and responsibility.
* **Emotional Boundaries:** Point out melodramatic, confessional, or savior-framed framing that weakens credibility.

**Closing Paragraph**

* **Narrative Synthesis:** Evaluate how effectively the conclusion integrates prior experiences into a coherent professional direction.
* **Forward Readiness:** Confirm the ending signals preparedness for medical training without sounding entitled or grandiose.
* **Tone Discipline:** Encourage grounded confidence and specificity instead of abstract mission statements.
