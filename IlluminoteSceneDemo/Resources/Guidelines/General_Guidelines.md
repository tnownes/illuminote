# General Personal Statement Guidelines

## Core Principles
1. **Show, Don't Tell**: Applicants should illustrate their qualities through concrete anecdotes and experiences rather than explicitly stating "I am empathetic."
2. **Professional Motivation**: The essay must clearly answer "Why this profession?" It should track a clear genesis of their interest.
3. **Growth and Reflection**: Every experience mentioned should conclude with an insight. What did the applicant learn? How did it change their worldview or approach to patient/client care?
4. **Authenticity**: The tone should be professional but highly personal. It should not sound like an academic textbook or a resume listed in paragraph form.

## Structure
- **Introduction**: Needs a strong hook. Avoid cliché quotes or childhood dreams unless they are directly tied to a sophisticated adult understanding of the profession.
- **Body Paragraphs**: 2-3 core anecdotes. Focus on times the applicant demonstrated resilience, leadership, or deep empathy.
- **Conclusion**: Synthesize the experiences. Look to the future. Why are they ready *now*?

## Red Flags
- Complaining about challenging coursework or blaming others for poor grades.
- Disparaging other professions (e.g., saying "I want to be a doctor because nurses don't diagnose").
- Using overly complex vocabulary (SAT words) where simple, direct language is better.
